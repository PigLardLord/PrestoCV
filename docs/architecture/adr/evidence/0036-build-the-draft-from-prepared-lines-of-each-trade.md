# Evidence for ADR 0036: Build the draft from prepared lines of each trade that the person confirms

The measurements behind [ADR 0036](../0036-build-the-draft-from-prepared-lines-of-each-trade.md). Every input was invented: no person's data was used. The ADR says what was decided and why; this file keeps the numbers it rests on, as they were measured.

- **What the on-device model does well and badly.** Probes of 2026-11-06 on a Mac mini, with invented inputs in Italian and English (electrician, cook, iOS developer, warehouse worker, shop assistant), one greedy run each:
  - **Choosing.** Given five ESCO occupations as its only allowed values, it chose the right one for 7 of 7 inputs. Given the O*NET occupations of the official crosswalk, it chose right in 6 of 6. It answered in the person's language in 7 of 7, with English source text in the prompt too. A request took 0.4 to 2.9 s.
  - **Writing the lines of a trade.** Asked for duties from ESCO skills, it copied the skill titles ("Strip wire."). Asked from O*NET tasks, it translated them word for word, in the style of a US job ad, once in the third person.
  - **Knowing a market.** Asked for norms with none given, it left the list empty in 6 of 6 drafts. Made to give some, 7 of 24 were real names, and none of the 8 for the Italian electrician and cook. Given a curated list of each market's core norms, the norms were right: CEI 64-8 and D.M. 37/2008 for an Italian electrician, HACCP for a cook, BS 7671 for a British electrician.
  - **Software and tools.** Its own software list for an iOS developer (Swift, SwiftUI, Xcode) was better than ESCO's for a software developer, which lists VBScript, Perl and COBOL and has Swift and Xcode only as optional. For the trades its tools were weak.
  - **Keeping the person's words.** It dropped a dish the person named, then changed its name in the next round; in another probe it added team leadership and "Senior".
  - **Repeats.** In one list of allowed values, 4 of 6 choices were the same item.
- **Fitting the lines to the person.** Two more probes on the same Mac, with five invented lines per trade and invented people:
  - **Every line rewritten.** Asked to rewrite the five lines of a job with what the person wrote, it put the person's words in the right line, with sense, for 7 of 10 people (greedy), 1.4 to 4.9 s a request. It also spread them into lines where they make no sense ("Collaborazione con l'impasto durante il servizio"), lost a line and repeated another ("Inventario." twice), switched to the first person, and for both shop assistants only replaced words with synonyms. Three sampled runs per person differed mostly by synonyms that read worse ("Took money at the counter and shut the cash box").
  - **A check of words is not a check of sense.** "Controllo della qualità e della freschezza degli impasti e del forno a legna" has only words of its line and of the person's text.
  - **One line at a time.** Asked to choose the line the person's text is about and rewrite only that one, it returned the line unchanged for 11 of 15 people and the person's text in place of the line for 4; its choices were often not the line the text was about. Asked to write one line of its own, it returned the person's text as written for 9 of 9 Italian people, and in English it added a claim ("managed stock efficiently") or lost the person's words in 4 of 5 answers. The guardrails refused one ordinary request ("led the migration from UIKit to SwiftUI"). A request took 0.6 to 1.4 s.
  - **A strict filter on the first probe.** Keeping a rewritten line only if it starts as its line does, holds a part of the person's text word for word, and has no word from neither, kept a line for 4 of 10 people in the greedy runs ("Installazione di impianti fotovoltaici sui tetti dei capannoni."), each with sense, and no line without sense. It rejected every English fit, whose verbs were synonyms ("Created" for "Built"). In the sampled runs it let one line without sense through ("…gli standard del forno a legna"). It was designed on these answers, so its numbers hold only for them.
- **Correcting what the person wrote about a job.** Two probes on the same Mac, with 12 invented notes of a job in three languages:
  - **With the title and the years in the prompt,** 14 lines repeated them and 4 were in the first person.
  - **With only the person's note,** told to write one line per thing done, a repeated thing once, not in the first person, and to keep the names of machines and tools: 6 of 12 corrections were good when read by hand and 5 partial (fragments, a repeat kept). One was bad: it lost "cassa", and a check of the lost parts catches it. No line repeated the title or the years, none was in the first person, all were in the person's language, and a request took 0.8 to 2.4 s.
  - **With the trade's lines as examples of form,** it copied 18 of them although told never to, and added claims ("D.Lgs. 81/2008").
  - **The spell checker of macOS** changed "swiftui" to "swift" and "evry" to "very".
- **Splitting a free text into its parts.** Two probes with 14 invented free texts in three languages, 47 sentences: the three shapes named in [the ADR's Context](../0036-build-the-draft-from-prepared-lines-of-each-trade.md#context), mixed ones (school, languages, licence, interests), a job with no punctuation, two jobs in one sentence, the presentation last.
  - **The cut.** NLTokenizer kept ". poi" and ". 2020-2022" inside one sentence. Cutting again after ".", "!" or "?" followed by a space fixed both.
  - **New job or same job.** Asked whether each sentence starts a job or goes on with the one before, the model called 9 of 15 sentences that start a job "same job", and got 2 right.
  - **The kind of each sentence.** Asked only for the kind (person, job, wanted job, school, skills, languages, licence, interests, details, other), with all the sentences of a text in one request, it was right for 39 of 47, and for every sentence in 9 of 14 texts, in 0.5 to 1.1 s a text. Half its misses put a job sentence under skills or other, and in one mixed text three kinds in a row were shifted by one. One request per sentence did no better: 40 of 47, every sentence right in 7 texts.
  - **The jobs grouped by code.** The model copied the workplace of each job sentence. Code started a job at the first job sentence, at a year, or at a workplace that the sentence contains. The jobs came out right in 9 of 12 texts. Each miss split one job in two (the model copied "Lì" and "Dort" as workplaces), and none merged two. The guardrails refused 2 ordinary sentences.
- **Correcting a presentation into a summary** (the same probe, 9 presentations).
  - The summaries had no first person, kept the person's language, fixed the spelling and added nothing, in 0.4 to 0.8 s.
  - One had a grammar slip ("Persona solare che piace stare con la gente").
  - One lost the person's fifteen years of experience. A check of the lost parts let it through, because another word of the same part was kept.
- **Speech on the device.** Probes of 2026-11-06 on a Mac mini (macOS 27), with invented answers in Italian and English read by the system's synthetic voices, so they measured the words, not accents:
  - SpeechTranscriber and DictationTranscriber support all six languages of the app, but their models are system assets that are downloaded on demand. On that Mac only English, and Italian for DictationTranscriber, were installed.
  - The numbers and years were right in every answer (15, 2012, 20 %). SpeechTranscriber in English was almost word for word. A transcription took 0.1 to 0.7 s.
  - DictationTranscriber garbled the words of the trades: "HACCP" became "CCCP", "UIKit" "Wiki", "CEI 64-8" "CEI 64 otto", "cook" "book"; and an employer's name: "Brightwire" became "Brightwater". It cut "No, non ce l'ho" to "No, non ce l'", and in English it heard "I can't waste" for "I cut waste", which turns the sentence around.
  - Given the trades' words as contextual strings, it got HACCP and UIKit right, and still not CEI 64-8, SwiftUI or BS 7671.
