# 0028. Check each personal claim of the AI draft again with the on-device model

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Related:** [ADR 0023](0023-ground-the-ai-draft-in-the-description.md), [ADR 0007](0007-each-package-owns-its-model.md), issues #202, #249

## Context

- **Why a second check.** The word rules of ADR 0023 decide whether a clause claims a fact. Three review rounds on #202 kept finding rarer variants of the same two families that the rules read as facts: wishes and denials. The last variant is text typed without accents. No word rule tells "seria" (serious) from "sería" (would be), or "wurde" (was) from "würde" (would): "Am liebsten wurde ich Spanisch lernen" keeps Spanisch, an open residual risk of ADR 0023. Later rounds added four families that need a reading of the relations inside a clause (whose words, whose title and place, whose verb) and a mark alone on its line, all open residual risks of ADR 0023.
- **What is at stake.** ADR 0023 (f): an invented or misattributed fact blocks the release.
- **What the owner asked (#249).** A second, independent check by the on-device model, measured first and then decided. A spike measured it on 2026-10-19; the owner decided to ship it in version 1 on 2026-10-24, with option (ii) below when the check gives no answer.

## Decision

- **Scope.** After the rules of ADR 0023, `CVGeneratorUseCase.generateCV` asks the claim check about the personal items that the grounded draft still keeps: the skills, the languages, the interests, the headline title and the place (`ClaimCandidates`). It then orders the jobs and validates the draft, as before.
- **What stays out.** Employers, schools, dates, certifications and prose stay with the entry rules of ADR 0023: the model judges them worse than the rules do (50 to 66 false drops on the contract in the spike).
- **An item stays only when both checks keep it.** The check can only withdraw (`ClaimWithdrawal`); a language goes with its level.
- **How the calls are made.** The candidates go to Apple's on-device model (`FoundationModelsClaimChecker`) in batches of at most 8, one batch after the other (`ClaimCheckLimits.standard`), each with a fixed instruction, greedy sampling and structured output (`@Generable`). In a batch the items are numbered from 1. For each item, the model copies the sentence that mentions it, then answers whose the item is (the writer, someone else, or not mentioned) and how the sentence states it (as true, wished, conditional or denied). An item is a fact only when it is the writer's and stated as true. A verdict that the withdrawal does not receive withdraws its item, so the check fails closed.
- **The answer is trusted for nothing it does not show (`ClaimAnswer`).**
  - It must number the items of the batch 1 to n, each once, in any order. An answer that skips, repeats, misnumbers, adds or leaves out an item cannot be matched to the candidates: it is no answer.
  - A fact stands only on a quote. The quote must be words of the description, as they run in it or in their order inside one of its sentences (the model shortens "I speak English, German and Spanish" to "I speak German"), and it must contain the item as whole words ("Java" is not in "JavaScript"). It must also leave out nothing that withdraws the claim. The clause the quote stands in, from the comma or semicolon before it to the one after it and never beyond a sentence, may hold no word of negation, wish, condition or future (`Vocabulary.withdrawals`, the modality lists but for ability and part) that the quote does not hold itself, and every word between the first and the last word of the quote is in that clause. So "I speak German" is no quote of "I do not speak German", "I speak English, not German" or "I speak German if needed", and "speak German" is none of them either; but "I speak German" is a quote of "I would like to learn Spanish, and I speak German", whose wish is in another clause. A fact whose quote is empty, is not in the description, does not contain the item or leaves out such a word is judged `notMentioned`: that one item is withdrawn. This is a verdict of the model that the check refuses to believe, not a failure of the call, so the draft does not become unchecked: the check only ever removes.
  - Another stance (a wish, a denial, someone else's, not mentioned) stands as the model gave it, whatever its quote: it already withdraws the item.
- **A time limit (the owner's decision, 2026-10-25).** The whole check has 60 seconds (`ClaimCheckLimits.time`). It races a timer: at the end of the time the batch in flight is cancelled and the check gives no answer, below. The check judged 36 items in 57 seconds, so 60 seconds is about 36 to 37 judged items, and six to twelve times the 5 seconds of a realistic draft of 5 to 9 (the measurement below). The limit replaces the model's own failure with many items in one call, which came late and silently (Alternatives considered, "One call per draft"). The batch size and the limit are defaults, not optima.
- **When the check gives no answer (option (ii)).** A failed call, a refusal of the model's guardrail, a batch whose answer has not the length of the batch, an answer that does not number its items 1 to n and a check that does not end within its time are the same path. The draft keeps its jobs, schools, dates and prose, and every item of the five kinds is withdrawn. `GeneratedDraft.personalItemsUnchecked` is true, and the editor's notice adds a line under the reminder to check the draft: "We could not check your skills, languages, interests, job title and place, so they are left blank. Add them yourself." There is no retry and never a fallback to the rules alone. The line is shown only then: a check that ran and withdrew some items says nothing more.
- **Never "no draft" for a check that gave no answer.** An unchecked draft may be empty, when the description holds nothing but personal items: it is still a draft, opened with the notice that says what is left blank, because a refusal that repeats on the same text would otherwise leave the person with no draft, ever (option (i), below). `CVGeneratorUseCase` validates the draft the rules grounded in that case, not the one the withdrawal left, so it reports an empty result only when the rules left nothing. A check that ran and withdrew everything is a different case: its draft is empty and is reported as empty (`emptyResult`), as the rules alone would have.
- **Cancellation** passes through as `CancellationError`, and gives no draft.
- **Where it lives.** The check, its candidates, the withdrawal and the result type are in the `CVGeneratorAI` package. The package imports no other package (ADR 0007): the app's `ViewModelFactory` gives the use case its `FoundationModelsClaimChecker`. The notice is an `EditorNotice` case chosen in `AIDraft`, outside the views; the banner only shows the lines it is given.
- **Tests.**
  - The unit tests of the use case and of the withdrawal use a fake check, so they need no model: they run on any Mac (the legacy target is Apple-only, ADR 0012). The app's unit tests use a fake check too, on every simulator, iOS 26.5 included.
  - `ClaimCheckEvaluation` runs the real model on Apple hosts, only when asked (`PRESTOCV_CLAIM_EVAL`), over the sets below, three times each, and fails on any false keep or on verdicts that differ between runs. Its part (e) runs drafts of 22, 32 and 42 personal items, the size of a long CV, through the batches and the time limit.
  - `ClaimAnswerTests` pin how an answer is read; `FoundationModelsClaimCheckerTests` pin the instruction (by its SHA-256, to be updated on purpose together with a new measurement) and the layout of the prompt; `ClaimWithdrawalTests` cover the batches, the time limit and the cancellation with a fake check that hangs.
  - The contract of ADR 0023 still judges the rules alone, so its known issues stay known issues of the rules; (a), composed, fails on any item of theirs that both checks keep. The mark alone on its line inside a quote left that list: a rule of ADR 0023 (c) now holds it.
  - Debug builds only: the launch arguments `-PrestoCVUITestsScriptedDraft nurse` (a scripted answer of the model, for simulators that cannot run it) and `-PrestoCVUITestsClaimCheck fail` (a check that throws) let the UI tests and the end-to-end campaign reach the unchecked draft. No release path reads them.

## The measurement

- **Where it ran.** The Mac mini, macOS 27.0.1, with Apple Intelligence on, on 2026-10-25, with the instruction of `FoundationModelsClaimChecker` (v7c, unchanged since 2026-10-24), the rules of the same commit, batches of 8 and the time limit of 60 seconds. No other host can run it yet: the iOS 26.5 simulator has no on-device model (every call ends in `assetsUnavailable`), and the MacBook has no Apple Intelligence. The code uses `GenerationOptions(samplingMode: .greedy)`, the name Xcode 27.1 asks for; the runs of 2026-10-24 used its old name, `sampling:`, and one more run with the new name gave the same verdicts, quotes and refusals for all 754 drafts.
- **The sets** (`ClaimCheckEvaluation`, `AdversarialClaims`).
  - (a) **The contract.** Every row of `GroundingContractTests` whose draft holds a personal item: 571 drafts and 890 items. The items a row keeps count as claimed (475); the items it drops count as not claimed.
  - (b) **The adversarial set.** 102 sentences with 141 items (57 claimed), in the six languages: the 77 of the spike, and 25 added while the instruction was tuned, for likings beside wishes, obligations, questions, lists under a heading and wishes to improve.
  - (c) **The spike's held-out set.** 42 sentences with 56 items (22 claimed). The instruction of this ADR was tuned with it in view, so it is now a regression set.
  - (d) **The new held-out set.** 42 sentences with 66 items (32 claimed), in the six languages, written before their first run and never used to choose the instruction.
  - (e) **Long drafts.** Three drafts of 22, 32 and 42 personal items (96 items, 78 claimed), the size of a long CV, for the batches and the time limit. The rules leave 16, 26 and 36 of them to the check.
- **Two modes.** Alone, the check judges every item of the set, in batches of 8 and without the time limit, as when the instruction is tuned. Composed, it judges only what the rules keep, through `ClaimWithdrawal` with the limits of the product, and an item stays when both keep it.
- **How the instruction evolved from the spike.** Measured on 2026-10-24, with one call for the whole draft; the instruction has not changed since, and the batches did not change what a call is asked. "Known issues kept" counts the rows among the known issues of the rules whose item the check keeps alone: on the product path each would be a false keep. ## Consequences

- **What it closes.** On the product path, no false keep on the 1,249 items of the five sets. That covers the families that the word rules cannot read: text typed without accents, the other variants of mood measured, whose words, whose title and place, whose verb, and the relatives of place. The mark alone on its line inside a quote is closed by a rule of ADR 0023 (c), not by the check, which keeps it alone.
- **What stays open.** The proof of a quote reads words, not meaning: it knows the negations, wishes, conditions and futures of the lists of the rules (ADR 0023), in the six languages, and no others. A claim withdrawn by a word that is not on them, or by one beyond the comma of its own clause, is not seen by the proof; there the verdict is the model's own, and the check only removes. A message with a lone symbol and no closing mark of its own (ADR 0023, Consequences): neither the rules nor the check tell it from a quote that the symbol closes.
- **What it costs.**
  - **Latency.** It grows with the number of items judged, and faster than that number: 1.4 s for a draft with one item, about 5 s for 5 to 9 items (the size of the real drafts of #202), 14 s for 22, 31 s for 32 and 57 s for the 36 items judged in a draft of 42. It comes on top of the 7 to 12 s that generation takes on the same Mac.
  - **The limit.** The time of 60 seconds is reached at about 36 to 37 judged items. A CV with more personal items than that takes the path of a check that gave no answer: no personal items, and the notice. The largest draft measured, with 42 items, took 57 to 59.4 s for the check and did not time out; there is little room beyond it.
  - **Losses.** 65 of the 475 claimed personal items of the contract (13.7%) are withdrawn on the product path. 32 (6.7%) are withdrawn by a verdict or by the quote proof; 33 are items of the 30 drafts whose check gave no answer (below). A withdrawn item is left out of the draft, never changed. The largest family of verdicts is still interests after "I like" in English (14 items), read as a wish in some drafts and not in others.
  - **Drafts with no answer.** 30 of the 286 contract drafts the check is called on (10.5%) give no answer, 3 of 42 in (b), 0 of 12 in (c) and 1 of 16 in (d); none of the 24 contract drafts of 5 to 9 judged items does. 28 of the 30 contract drafts are answers that do not number their items 1 to n, and 2 are refusals of the guardrail. 27 of the 30 have one candidate, 27 of the 197 drafts with one candidate (13.7%): asked about one item, the model gives one verdict for each sentence that mentions it, so it repeats the number 1 or numbers an extra item. Under greedy sampling the same text gives the same answer every time, so it repeats on retry; that is why there is none. These drafts open with the notice, as option (ii) decides.
  - **Refusals.** The guardrail refuses some benign sentences, 2 of the contract's 571 drafts on the product path. They give a draft without the personal items, with the notice.
- **The strict reading of the answer is a decision with this cost.** It is the safer default: an answer that cannot be matched to the candidates is not believed. Two ways to give the drafts above their items back are open for the owner, and neither is taken (Alternatives considered): a lenient reading, and an answer whose length the schema fixes. Both need the full measurement again, in both modes.
- **The check is not a replacement for the rules.** Alone, it keeps 76 items of the contract that the rules drop. It is safe only behind them, and the contract of ADR 0023 still judges the rules alone.
- **Model versions.** The model changes with the OS. `ClaimCheckEvaluation` must run again, every set in both modes, on each OS release that may change the model. It ran on macOS 27.0.1 only. An iOS 26 device, which may carry an older model, is the owner's check before release, since the iOS 26.5 simulator has no model: the figures here are measured on macOS 27.0.1 only.
- **Determinism.** Greedy decoding gave the same verdicts and quotes in every repeated run. Verdicts still depend on the other items of the same batch. A composed call holds fewer items than an alone one, and "Parlo inglese e vorrei imparare il russo" keeps "inglese" alone but withdraws it composed, when "russo" is no longer asked. On the product path no such change kept a false item: the composed false keeps are 0 in every set.
- **Tuning.** The instruction was chosen on (a), (b) and (c). Only (d) measures it on wordings it was not tuned on: there the check has no false keep, one false drop (a garbled copy of a quote, "leese gern Krimis") and one draft with no answer.
- **The rules cost more with many skills.** In part (e) the rules of ADR 0023 took 0.02 s for 22 items, 0.2 s for 32 and 1.06 s for 42. That is not the check, but it is part of the time of a long draft.

## Alternatives considered

- **More word rules.** Rejected: three review rounds found new variants each time, and accents cannot be recovered by rule.
- **The model alone, replacing the rules.** Rejected by the measurement: alone it keeps futures, job searches and other people's facts that the rules drop.
- **Including employers and schools.** Rejected by the measurement: 50 to 66 false drops on the contract.
- **No draft when the check gives no answer (option (i)).** Rejected by the owner: a guardrail refusal repeats on the same text, so the person could never get a draft from that description.
- **One call per draft.** The first version of this decision. Rejected after the review of #249 measured it: above about 5 items the model withdrew every skill and showed no notice, it kept items with quotes that did not contain them, and a draft of 60 items overflowed the 8,192-token window after 206 s. Batches keep each answer short.
- **One call per item.** It avoids verdicts that change with the other items, at about 1.4 s per call, so about 10 s for a realistic draft of 7 items against 5 s in batches. Not taken: batches already give no false keep on the product path, and one call per item would double the wait for a realistic draft. Its gain, fewer losses such as the interests after "I like", is not measured; it stays the next step if those losses matter in use.
- **Another batch size or time.** 8 candidates and 60 seconds are the defaults the review asked the owner to choose. No other size was measured: a smaller batch makes more calls, a larger one comes closer to the failures of one call per draft.
- **A lenient reading of the answer (not taken).** Ignore the entries numbered outside 1 to n and read repeated numbers together (an item stays only when every entry that names it is a backed fact), asking only that every item has an entry. It would give back most of the 30 drafts above. It weakens the guarantee that an answer is matched to its candidates, which the quote proof (a fact needs a quote that contains the item itself) only partly makes up for.
- **An answer of fixed length (not taken).** A dynamic generation schema with as many entries as candidates would stop the model from adding entries, so the strict reading would hold without the losses. It changes what the model is asked to produce, so it needs a full measurement, in both modes.
