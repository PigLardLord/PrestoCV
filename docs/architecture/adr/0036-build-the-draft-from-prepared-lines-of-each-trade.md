# 0036. Build the draft from prepared lines of each trade that the person confirms

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-06
- **Amended:** 2026-11-19: from v1.1.x the on-device model extracts from a free text the facts that code checks against the text, the person confirms each one and the rest is asked, and the packs also hold a whole CV of each trade, published as open data (Context, "The drafts of 1.0.1" and "The facts probe"; Decision, "ADR 0031, ADR 0034 and ADR 0035"; Consequences, "Amended 2026-11-19"; Alternatives)
- **Related:** [ADR 0031](0031-let-the-ai-draft-write-around-the-facts.md), [ADR 0033](0033-change-the-legacy-app-only-to-fix-the-published-version.md), [ADR 0023](0023-ground-the-ai-draft-in-the-description.md), [ADR 0028](0028-check-each-personal-claim-of-the-ai-draft-again.md), [ADR 0034](0034-read-a-text-that-is-already-a-cv-with-the-import.md), [ADR 0026](0026-keep-the-core-portable-to-android.md), [ADR 0027](0027-import-a-cv-from-its-text-with-rules.md), ADR 0035 (proposed in #374), epic #192, issues #113, #167, #196, #205, #206, #207, #208, #210, #212, #220, #228, #229, #230, #253, #265, #272, #273, #314, #366, #368, #507

## Context

- **The v1 draft writes from a free text on the device.** Since ADR 0031 the on-device model reads the person's description, extracts its facts, then writes a summary, duties and skills around them, and rules remove what states a fact of its own. Tuning it (#354) gave less with each round. After trying the 1.0.1 builds (2026-11-05 and 2026-11-06), the maintainer found the drafts thin and not credible.
- **The maintainer's goal (2026-11-06).** From a few lines, a CV that reads like a credible, ready example of the person's trade, which the person then completes with their real data. It shows how a strong CV is written. Not every line has to be the person's own yet, but everything the person enters must be in it. The maintainer chose:
  - guided questions per job: the facts go straight into their fields, the AI proposes what is typical, and the person confirms it with a tap; legal qualifications are yes/no questions;
  - Apple's Private Cloud Compute on top, with consent that says where the text goes (#253).
- **The maintainer's limit (2026-11-06).** The CVs of one trade must not all read the same because their lines were prepared in advance: the model should be able to rephrase the lines by what the person wrote.
- **The person's own text (the maintainer, 2026-11-06).**
  - People often write in an incomplete or wrong way. The draft should correct what they write, remove the repeats and give it the language of a CV, not keep it word for word.
  - A free text can hold anything. Most likely it is a presentation and some jobs, a presentation only, or jobs only, and then the summary has to be inferred.
- **The plan of 2026-10-17 (epic #192).**
  - The spike #229 scored ESCO for CV suggestions, blind, on 12 occupations in three languages. A person kept 43% of the raw ESCO items (45 were wrong), and 97% of the items a strong model wrote from ESCO. The small on-device model is not good enough to write such items; it may run checks. Seniority changes the phrases (42% depend on the band) far more than the skills.
  - The packs of #205: per occupation and market, written by a strong model at batch prices, with skills and CV phrases by band and "no facts in phrases", checked before publishing and reviewed every month. They are signed static files that the app syncs in the background (#230: "No user text, occupation or identifier is sent, and nothing is logged per user").
  - Curated corrections as data (#206), offline matching of the title (#207: the pack aliases matched 38 or 39 of 39 inputs, the ESCO search put the right occupation first for 13), a suggestion engine with no model call (#208), the confirm UI (#210), experience suggestions (#220), and demand data from job ads (#228).
  - The ADR planned by #196, with the maintainer's decision of 2026-10-25: tools may be proposed; certifications, licences and work experience never.
- **What the probes found (2026-11-06).** Probes of the on-device model on a Mac mini, with invented inputs in Italian and English and, for the split, in three languages; [the evidence appendix](evidence/0036-build-the-draft-from-prepared-lines-of-each-trade.md) has every number:
  - **The first two probes, from ESCO and from O*NET through the official crosswalk.** The model chose the right occupation from a list every time, in the person's language. It did not write the lines of a trade: it copied ESCO's skill titles and translated O*NET's tasks word for word. Asked for a market's norms, it gave none, or few real ones; given a curated list, it chose them right. Its software for an iOS developer was better than ESCO's, its tools for the trades weak. It dropped and changed the person's words, and added claims.
  - **The third probe, every line of a job rewritten with what the person wrote.** It fit the person's words into the right line for 7 of 10 people, but also into lines where they make no sense, and a check of words cannot judge sense. A strict filter kept a line for 4 of 10 people, each with sense, and rejected every English fit.
  - **The fourth probe, one line at a time.** It returned the line unchanged, or the person's text as written, and chose lines the text was not about.
  - **The job-text probe, correcting what the person wrote about a job.** With only the person's note, 6 of 12 corrections were good, 5 partial and 1 bad. Given the trade's lines as examples of form, it copied 18 of them and added claims.
  - **The split of a free text.** Asked for the kind of each sentence, it was right for 39 of 47 sentences and for every sentence in 9 of 14 texts. Code grouped the jobs right in 9 of 12 texts; each miss split one job in two, and none merged two.
  - **A presentation corrected into a summary.** The summaries added nothing and kept the person's language; one of 9 lost the person's years of experience, and a check of the lost parts let it through.
- **The sources.**
  - ESCO (CC BY 4.0): occupations and skills in every EU language, a free API that allows one call every 2 s, and outdated software skills.
  - O*NET 31.0 (CC BY 4.0): tasks as full sentences, in English only, and a software list. It has had no hand tools since release 30.3. The official ESCO–O*NET crosswalk found O*NET occupations for 7 of 7 inputs.
  - ROME 4.0 and the "Mots-clés de CV par métier" (Licence Ouverte 2.0): typed CV keywords for 1,911 French jobs, tools, norms and certifications among them.
  - Not usable: Lightcast Open Skills (free for non-profit use only) and BERUFENET (the Bundesagentur für Arbeit keeps exclusive rights).
  - The O*NET and France Travail web APIs need a key. None of the sources offers GraphQL.
- **New occupations.** The sources add occupations only with their releases: O*NET 31.0 has no "prompt engineer", and the ESCO search for it returns the theatre prompter first. The maintainer asked (2026-11-06) to regenerate the data every one or two months, so that new trades arrive.
- **Two more ways in (the maintainer, 2026-11-06).** Besides the free text, the person can scan a document, or talk with the coach: a chat of questions, answered in writing or by voice.
  - The scan is designed already. ADR 0027 (row F3) reads the camera, photos and scans on the device, with VisionKit's document camera and Vision's `RecognizeDocumentsRequest`; #167 and #265 plan it for the import of v1.1.
  - #272 plans a guided interview, by voice or on screen, whose script, questions, checks and mapping to a draft live in the portable core; App Intents and Siri are adapters of it. #273 plans one command layer for the screens, Siri, an in-app chat and other channels.
- **Speech on the device.** Probes of 2026-11-06 on a Mac mini, with invented answers in Italian and English read by the system's synthetic voices, so they measured the words, not accents. SpeechTranscriber and DictationTranscriber support all six languages of the app, with models downloaded on demand. Numbers and years were right in every answer, but the dictation garbled the words of the trades ("HACCP" became "CCCP") and an employer's name, and contextual strings fixed some of them, not all. [the evidence appendix](evidence/0036-build-the-draft-from-prepared-lines-of-each-trade.md) has the details.
- **The drafts of 1.0.1 (the maintainer, 2026-11-18).** After trying them again, the maintainer found the drafts still too simple and plain ("sembrano scritti da un bambino": they read as if a child wrote them). Their idea: whole CVs of each trade, written in advance by much stronger models, which the app fills with the dates, names and facts taken from what the person wrote, like the packs above.
- **The facts probe (2026-11-19).** On a Mac mini, with invented free texts in Italian for three trades, the on-device model filled an answer schema built from the trade's labels, and code checked each value against the text. On three held-out texts, set aside and fingerprinted before the last run, code kept 1 wrong fact and dropped 11 values; a text took 9 to 36 s. The wrong fact was a goal that no word of the text could confirm. The [evidence appendix](evidence/0036-build-the-draft-from-prepared-lines-of-each-trade.md) has the numbers.

## Decision

- **The draft holds three kinds of content.**
  - **The person's own:** their answers to the questions (title, employer, years, place, what they did, the yes/no qualifications), set by code word for word in the field they belong to, and the sentences of their free text as written, in the part where they placed them. No model changes them: a correction is only proposed, and they stay saved with the CV.
  - **Proposed:** a line of a pack, a correction of the person's text, or a text of Private Cloud Compute, marked as a proposal. It enters the CV only when the person confirms it with a tap; they can edit it after. A confirmed correction takes the place of the person's text in the CV, and the text as written stays saved.
  - **To complete:** a slot with a typed placeholder (an employer "Company 1", a figure "[X] %" with its unit, a period) and a coach's tip on what to write there. A placeholder is not CV content: the CV stores the empty slot with its kind, never the placeholder text, and nothing prints it. A proposed line with a slot can be confirmed only once its slot is filled.
  - **Before the export** the app lists the proposals not confirmed, the slots left and the sentences not placed. The export does not wait for them (the maintainer, 2026-11-06), and what is left is not printed.
- **Every value keeps its origin** (#196): written, answered, accepted from a pack (as it is or fitted on the device), accepted as a correction of the person's text, accepted from Private Cloud Compute, or imported. An accepted pack line keeps its pack item id and its source (#208).
- **The knowledge of a trade is prepared, not generated on the device.** The packs of #205 hold, per occupation and market:
  - **The lines of the experience section** (#205's phrases), written in the market's language as lines of a CV, by band of seniority (#208), with no fact: no number, no name, no employer, no licence. A pack holds more lines per band than a CV shows, and each line in two or three wordings. The pipeline's strong model writes them from ESCO, from the O*NET tasks of the occupation through the official crosswalk, from the ROME data for fr-FR, and from the curated data (#206). An O*NET task is a meaning to write from, never a text to translate.
  - **Achievement templates:** a line with typed slots ("Reduced waste by [X] % in [period]"), proposed as a line, with its slots to complete.
  - **Summary templates,** whose slots code fills from the person's own facts (title, years) and the lines they confirmed.
  - **The tools in two lists,** software and equipment (machines and hand tools). O*NET has software only; the equipment comes from the curated data, the ROME keywords and the strong model.
  - **The norms of the market,** only from the curated data (#206), never from a model.
  - **The legal qualifications and licences** of the trade in that market, from the curated data, asked as yes/no questions and never proposed (#196). A yes is the person's own answer.
  - **The checks before publishing:** those of #205, plus no fact in a line or a template, no word-for-word translation of a source task, every norm and qualification from the curated data, and no repeated line.
- **The packs are regenerated on a schedule.**
  - A batch runs every one or two months (the maintainer, 2026-11-06). It first compares the inputs of each pack with those of its last published version, which calls no model, and regenerates only the packs whose inputs changed: a release of a source, a change of the curated data, a new title from job ads, a new prompt. A run with no change costs nothing. A paid run stays within the budget of Cost below.
  - The monthly diff review and the 5% sample of #205 stay. When a check fails, or the budget ends before a pack is done, the last published pack stays, and the next run that has a budget tries that pack again, since its inputs still differ.
  - The output is signed static files that the app syncs in the background (#230). Where the batch runs is for the hosting ADR (#205). The keys of the sources that need one stay in the pipeline's secrets.
- **New occupations come from public data, never from the app.** The app sends nothing (#230). A new title comes from job ads (#228, once their terms allow it) or from the review, and a person approves it as a curated alias or a new pack (#206). Until then, a title with no pack goes to the closest occupation (#207), which the person sees and can change.
- **Two people of one trade get different CVs** (the maintainer's limit above), from four sources:
  - their own text of each job, as written or corrected, in its entry;
  - the lines their words pick: code ranks a pack's lines by the person's words and band (#208), so different texts can pick different lines;
  - the wording: code picks one of a line's wordings from the CV's identifier, so another CV of the trade may read another, and one CV keeps its own;
  - the lines fitted to what the person wrote, by the on-device model and by Private Cloud Compute, below.

  What code picks repeats: the same pack and the same input, the CV's identifier included, give the same lines and wordings (#208). The fitting need not repeat: the deadline, a refusal of the guardrails or another model version can leave other lines. So the draft's proposals, the fitted lines included, are saved with the CV when the draft is built; opening the CV again shows them as saved and calls no model. Only building the draft again, when the person asks for it, runs the fitting again. No sampling makes the CVs differ.
- **On the device, code builds the draft; the model chooses, gives the kind of a sentence, and writes only through a check.**
  - Code asks the questions per job and the market's yes/no questions, matches the occupation offline (#207), sets the answers word for word, and ranks the pack's proposals (#208). This works on every device, offline and without Apple Intelligence.
  - Where the on-device model is available, it may choose among values that code gives it: the occupation among the matcher's candidates when the matcher is unsure, and the pack lines that fit what the person wrote for a job. Its answer schema allows only those values (`GenerationGuide.anyOf`), and code removes repeats. A choice only reorders the proposals.
  - It may also fit the proposed lines of a job to what the person wrote, in one greedy request per job, once when the draft is built, with no retry. Code keeps a rewritten line only if it starts with the first word of its pack line, holds a part of the person's text word for word, and has no word that is in neither its pack line nor the person's text (the provenance check of #113). Otherwise the pack line stays. A failure, a refusal of the guardrails or the deadline leaves all the pack lines of that job.
  - A kept line is a proposal like any other, with its pack item id; the person confirms it or not. Apart from these lines and the corrections below, the on-device model writes no text that enters the CV.
- **The guided start opens with a free text, which can hold anything** (the maintainer, 2026-11-06).
  - A text that is already a CV goes to the import, as ADR 0034 says.
  - Code cuts the text into sentences: NLTokenizer, then again after ".", "!" or "?" followed by a space.
  - Where the on-device model is available, it gives the kind of each sentence (person, job, wanted job, school, skills, languages, licence, interests, details, other) in one greedy request per text, whose answer schema has exactly one of these values per sentence. Then, in one request per job sentence, it copies the workplace that the sentence names. Without the model, or after a failure, a refusal or the deadline, the sentences start unplaced, except those the import's rules place (ADR 0034).
  - Code groups the job sentences into jobs: a job starts at the first job sentence, at a year, or at a workplace that the sentence contains, and a workplace that it does not contain is ignored. In doubt it starts a new job, and so does a failure, a refusal or the deadline of that sentence's request: a wrong split shows as two jobs that the person joins, while a wrong merge would hide a job.
  - Before the questions, the person sees the sentences under their headings (the presentation, each job, school, skills, …), moves one with a tap, and then confirms the split with one more tap. Only that confirmation places a sentence, whoever proposed its heading (the model or the import's rules): until then every sentence counts as not placed, and no draft is built from it. Nothing is dropped: a sentence that fits nowhere stays in a visible "not placed" list, saved with the CV and not printed. The quick add of #314 places a note the same way.
  - The questions of each job show the workplace, the years and the sentences found for it as answers to confirm.
  - A presentation with no jobs: the questions ask for the jobs, and the occupation is matched from the presentation (#207). Jobs with no presentation: the summary is inferred, below.
- **The person can also scan a document or answer the coach in a chat** (the maintainer, 2026-11-06). Every way in ends in the same three places: the import, the split of a free text, or the answers to the questions.
  - **A scan.** The document camera or a photo, read on the device by the extraction of ADR 0027 (row F3, #167). ADR 0034's rule reads the whole scan, every page together, never one page at a time: a CV's experience and its skills can sit on different pages. A scan that the rule reads as a CV goes to the import with all its pages. Any other scan, such as notes, a work certificate or a diploma, is a free text: the person sees the whole recognized text and can fix it, and then it goes through the split above. Nothing leaves the device.
  - **The chat.** The coach asks the questions of the guided start one at a time, and the person answers in writing or by voice. The chat and the screens are two views of one interview in the portable core (#272): the same questions, answers, storage and checks. So the same answers give the same draft, and the person can switch between the two.
    - The questions are written in code (the string catalog) and in the packs (the yes/no qualifications). No model writes or chooses a question.
    - An answer to a closed question (a workplace, the years, yes or no) is set by code word for word in its field, as on the screens. An answer that code cannot read as a year or as yes or no stays as text, marked for the person to fix.
    - An open answer ("Tell me about yourself and your work") is a free text: it goes through the split, and the person confirms it as above.
  - **Voice.**
    - Speech is recognized on the device only, by SpeechAnalyzer with SpeechTranscriber or DictationTranscriber. The audio is never saved or sent.
    - The recognizer gets the words of the person's trade from the pack (its tools, norms and titles) as contextual strings.
    - The transcript appears as the text of the answer, and the person can edit it before sending it. Only the text as sent counts, and it goes through the same checks and corrections as a written answer.
    - When the language's model is not on the device, the app offers to download it through the system. Until it is there, or for a language with no model on the device, the app offers no voice of its own. The keyboard's dictation is the system's, under the person's own settings, and works in every text field.
    - The coach may read the questions aloud with the system's speech synthesizer, on the device.
    - The app asks for the microphone, and for any permission the recognizer needs, only when the person first chooses voice.
  - **Where the code lives.** The interview's script is in the portable core, which gets only text (ADR 0026). The document camera is a view in the app's `Platform/` folder; the recognition of text and of speech are adapters of their own.
- **The person's text is corrected as a proposal** (the maintainer, 2026-11-06).
  - Where the on-device model is available, it corrects the sentences of each job in one greedy request, and the presentation in one more, once when the draft is built, with no retry: in the language of a CV, a repeated thing said once, not in the first person, the names of machines and tools kept. The request holds only the person's text and its language: no pack line, no title, no years.
  - Code keeps a correction only if it keeps a word of each clause of the person's text and, one by one, each number, number word and name in it; adds no number, name or job title that the text lacks; is not in the first person; is in the person's language; and copies no pack line. Otherwise, and after a failure, a refusal or the deadline, the person's text stays as written.
  - The app shows a correction beside the person's text: the person accepts it, edits it or keeps their own. The system's spell checker only suggests, and nothing applies it by itself. The corrections are saved with the CV, like the fitted lines.
  - With no presentation, the summary is proposed from a pack's summary template (#212), whose slots code fills: the title, the years (#366) and the skills or lines the person confirmed. From v1.2 Private Cloud Compute may write it instead, with the person's consent. Either is a proposal.
- **Private Cloud Compute writes only what a pack cannot hold** (v1.2, #253): a correction of what the person wrote, the pack lines of a job fitted to it, and a summary. Its corrections go through the same check as those of the device.
  - It runs only after the person's consent, which says where the text goes. The request carries what the person wrote for the draft and the occupation, never the contacts.
  - What it writes is proposed, under the same rule as a pack line: it states no fact the person did not give. ADR 0023 and ADR 0028 say what a fact is. The person's own text stays beside it.
- **ADR 0031, ADR 0034 and ADR 0035.** ADR 0031 stays the rule of the v1 draft in the legacy app, which takes only fixes of the published version (ADR 0033). The new targets do not extract facts from a free text with a model: the model only gives the kind of a sentence, the person confirms where it goes, and the facts come from the questions and from code. ADR 0034 stays for a text that is already a CV: the import reads it, and the AI only fills its gaps. ADR 0035 (proposed in #374) keeps its measurement for this draft. What it lets the draft infer (the core tools of the trade, its norms, the scope and seniority that the years give) is inferred in the packs and by Private Cloud Compute, never by the on-device model.

  **Amended 2026-11-19 (maintainer's decision, #507):** from v1.1.x the on-device model may extract from a free text the facts that code checks against the text, and the person confirms each one; what no word of the text can confirm is asked. The packs also hold a whole CV of each trade, which code fills with the confirmed facts. The text above stays as it was decided, and holds for v1.1.
  - **What the model may extract,** in one greedy request whose answer schema is built from the trade's labels (`DynamicGenerationSchema`): the person's name, contacts and city; each job's employer, role, start, end and kind of employer; each school's name, subject, place and year; the languages with their level; the interests; and a word of the text that shows the person's gender. Then it chooses the trade among those with a whole CV, and for each job the duties among the closed list of its role.
  - **What code keeps.** A value is kept only if it is a whole word of the text. A place, a date or a year is kept only when a sentence that names its employer or school says it, and never when it is part of a name. A range ("dal 2015 al 2019") sets the end, and a word for "now" means no end. The kind of employer that the employer's name says wins over the model's. A language level that names another language moves to that language. An interest needs a cue of free time. A flag of the trade (a certificate, a school of the trade) needs its evidence word, listed per trade in the pack. A gender word is kept only when code reads its ending; otherwise the app asks. In doubt code drops the value, and the questions ask for it.
  - **What is never extracted.** Goals, qualities, wishes, and any flag with no evidence word: no word of a text can confirm them, so the questions ask for them.
  - **What the person sees.** Every kept fact is an answer to confirm, as the questions of each job already show the workplace and the years. A legal qualification found this way is the answer "yes" to its question, still to confirm. The duties chosen from the closed list are proposals, like a pack line. Without the model, or after a failure, a refusal or the deadline, the questions ask for every fact.
  - **The whole CV of a trade,** per trade and market: a summary by band of seniority, each in two or more wordings; the lines of each role, those that need something the person stated (a station, a speciality) and the typical lines of the role; and skill groups whose items carry their condition and origin. The pipeline's strong model writes it under the pack checks above, and it holds no fact. Code fills it with the confirmed facts, keeps the lines whose conditions hold, and picks a wording by the CV's identifier, as for the lines above. Each line is a proposal that the person confirms.
  - **The open data.** The packs and the whole CVs are published in a public repository of their own, PrestoCV-data, under CC BY 4.0, with the attribution that the sources require: ESCO and O*NET under CC BY 4.0, the ROME data under Licence Ouverte 2.0.
  - **v1.1.x (January 2027):** the extraction and the whole CVs of the first trades. These CVs ship inside the app from one run of the pipeline; the scheduled regeneration and the background sync stay as above (#205, #230). The extraction ships only if, on new held-out invented texts, its check keeps no wrong fact.
- **The releases follow the [vision](../../product/vision.md).**
  - v1.1, the guided start, offline on every device: the free text and its split, the questions per job and the market's yes/no questions, the offline match of the occupation (#207), the skills, tools and norms of the packs as proposals (#208, #210), and the summary templates. Where the on-device model is available, it gives the kinds of the sentences and corrects the person's text. The corrections ship only if, on new invented inputs, their check keeps no correction that lost a fact. With them come the scan, with the import (#167), and the written chat as a second view of the questions; the person can speak through the keyboard's dictation.
  - v1.2: the lines of the experience section with their wordings, their fitting on the device, the achievement templates, the proposals per employer (#220) and Private Cloud Compute (#253), with consent. The fitting on the device ships only if, on new invented inputs, its check keeps no line without sense. The coach's own voice (it reads the questions and listens, on the device) ships only if a probe with recorded voices of people reading invented answers, in the six languages, gets every number and year right and turns no answer around (a "no" heard as a "yes", "can't" for "cut"); it measures the trade's words with and without the contextual strings, and the names of employers and places.
  - A pack may hold what a later release shows; the app shows it only from that release.
- **Targets.** The pull requests that create the targets of the packs, the matching, the suggestions and the draft add them to `modules.md` (#205, #207, #208, #220).

## Consequences

- **Easier.**
  - Every device gets credible lines of a trade, offline, and two people of one trade get different ones even with no model. The knowledge is checked once per pack instead of hoped for in each answer.
  - The person's words are never lost: they are saved as written, and a correction takes their place in the CV only when the person accepts it.
  - The draft is code over data: a fixture pack and the person's answers give one draft on every host, Linux included, with no model.
  - Three ways in, one draft: a text, a scan and the chat end in the same split and the same questions, so their checks are written once.
- **Harder.**
  - The batch and its review every one or two months. The curated norms and qualifications need a person who knows each market; a market without them gets no norms and no questions, never invented ones.
  - The packs grow with the lines, their wordings and the templates. The first pack run measures their size against #205's estimate of about 0.6 MB gzip per language.
  - The fitting on the device helps few people: the check that keeps it safe rejected every English fit of the probe, and it does not judge sense, so the person's confirmation stays the last check.
  - The person answers a few questions after writing their text: more taps, fewer surprises.
  - One more screen: the person checks the split of their text and confirms it. In the probe about one text in three needed a move.
  - The check of a correction reads facts, not grammar: a slip such as "Persona solare che piace stare con la gente" passes, so the person's eye stays the last check.
  - One more view in v1.1, the chat. In v1.2 the coach's voice brings the microphone permission and a model download per language.
  - A spoken answer can hold a wrong word, of the trade or not: in the probe the Italian dictation heard "CCCP" for HACCP, and the English one "can't" for "cut" and "Brightwater" for the employer Brightwire. The contextual strings fixed some words and not others, so the person's eye on the transcript stays the last check.
- **Cost (principle 7 of the [vision](../../product/vision.md)).**
  - The packs: about $175 for the first full run at batch prices (#205), whatever the number of users. The wordings add to the share of the lines; the first run measures the total within its budget. A later run pays only for the packs whose inputs changed, as the vision's v1.1 row says, and a run with no change costs nothing.
  - Hosting: static files on a free tier. With no users, 1,000 users or 100,000 users the cost stays 0 on a host whose bandwidth is not metered: 100,000 people who each fetch one language of about 0.6 MB make about 60 GB per run.
  - The device: free, the scan and the speech included. Private Cloud Compute: free under Apple's terms for Small Business Program developers below 2 million first-time downloads, with a daily quota per person.
  - Before revenue, each paid run needs a budget that the maintainer approves for it. Without one, or once it is spent, the run stops before its next paid call and the last packs stay in use. No paid run starts by the schedule alone.
- **Amended 2026-11-19.**
  - **Easier.** From a few lines, a draft that reads like a strong CV of the trade, and fewer things to type: a fact that the text states is shown as an answer to confirm.
  - **Harder.** A whole CV per trade and market to write and review; a trade without one gets the lines of the packs and the questions. A kept fact can still be wrong in a way that code cannot see, so the person's confirmation stays the last check. In the probe the extraction took 9 to 36 s a text, against about a second for the kinds of the sentences; the first build measures it on the slowest supported device.
  - **Cost.** The first whole CVs need a paid run of the pipeline, under the budget rule above.
  - **Checks.** A table-driven test of each check of an extracted value, with rows it must keep and rows it must drop: a place said in another sentence or inside a name, an end set by a range, a word for "now", a level that names another language, an interest with no cue of free time, a flag with no evidence word, and a gender word whose ending code cannot read. A test that a goal, a quality or a flag with no evidence word becomes a question and never a value. A test over a fixture of people of one trade that their whole CVs read differently.

## Alternatives considered

- **Tune the free-text draft further** (ADR 0031, #354). Each round gave less, and the small model cannot both extract the facts and write the lines of a trade.
- **Let the on-device model write the lines from source tasks in its prompt** (the second probe). It translated them word for word, in the style of a US job ad and in the third person, and changed the person's words.
- **Let the on-device model rewrite the lines freely** (the third probe). It fit the person's words into the right line for 7 of 10 people, but it also put them where they make no sense, lost and repeated lines, and replaced words with synonyms. A check of words cannot judge sense, so only the lines that pass the strict check are kept.
- **Make the CVs differ by sampling** (a temperature). The sampled runs differed by synonyms that read worse, and one line without sense passed the strict check. The variety comes from the packs' lines and wordings, and a CV keeps the proposals saved with it.
- **Ask the model for one line at a time** (the fourth probe). It returned the line unchanged or the person's text as written, chose lines the text was not about, and in English added claims.
- **Keep the person's text word for word.** The maintainer rejected it (2026-11-06): people write in an incomplete or wrong way, and a CV needs its own language.
- **Give the correction the trade's lines as examples of form** (the job-text probe). It copied 18 of them although told never to, and added claims.
- **Apply the spell checker by itself.** It changed "swiftui" to "swift" and "evry" to "very".
- **Let the model group the sentences into jobs.** It called 9 of 15 sentences that start a job "same job".
- **Ask for the kind of each sentence in its own request.** It was no better (40 of 47 against 39), fewer texts were fully right (7 against 9), and it costs one request per sentence.
- **Place the sentences without showing the split.** Only 9 of 14 texts were fully right, so about one text in three would put a sentence in the wrong place unseen.
- **Propose the raw ESCO lists** (#229). A person kept 43% of them, 45 were wrong, and the software skills are outdated.
- **Call the sources' APIs from the app,** so that the data is always current (the maintainer's first idea). The keys would ship in the app, ESCO allows one call every 2 s, and #207 promises that the app never calls ESCO. Packs regenerated every one or two months are as current as the sources, which release a few times a year.
- **Learn the missing titles from what the app sends.** Keeping what the app sends is collected data in Apple's privacy details ("Other User Content"), and it breaks the promise of #230. Job ads give new titles with no data of any person.
- **Write the whole draft with Private Cloud Compute.** It needs iOS 27 or later, a device with Apple Intelligence and the maintainer's entitlement, and it has a daily quota. The guided start must work offline on every device (v1.1).
- **A GraphQL service over the sources.** None of them offers one, and static packs need no query service.
- **Recognize speech on a server.** The audio would leave the device, which principle 3 of the vision rules out.
- **Let the model write or choose the coach's questions, or hold a free conversation.** Questions written in code are the same on every device, offline, and are reviewed once.
- **The older speech recognizer** (SFSpeechRecognizer). It had 5 languages on the device in the probe, while SpeechTranscriber supports all six of the app's.
- **The coach's own voice in v1.1.** With synthetic voices the Italian dictation garbled words of the trades, and no human voice was measured. The keyboard's dictation covers v1.1.
- **Keep extracting no fact with a model** (this ADR before 2026-11-19). The person would type again every date and name that their text already holds, while in the facts probe every value that code checked against a word of the text was right.
- **Let the on-device model also choose the goals and qualities** (the facts probe). The one wrong fact on the held-out texts was a goal that no word of the text confirms; a question asks for it instead.
